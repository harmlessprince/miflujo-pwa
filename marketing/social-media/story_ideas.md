# MiFlujo Story Ideas

Use these ideas for short fiction, social videos, carousel posts, skits, testimonials, and founder-led content. The goal is to make readers or watchers feel recognized first, then show MiFlujo as the tool that turns bank-statement confusion into clear financial intelligence.

Primary CTA:

> Visit https://miflujo.taoforge.org to get early access.

## Creative Principle

Every story should begin with an emotion people already know:

- "I earn money, but I cannot explain where it goes."
- "I am doing my best, but everyone still needs something from me."
- "My business is selling, so why am I always short?"
- "I thought I was careful until I saw the pattern."
- "I do not need judgement. I need clarity."

MiFlujo should enter the story as a calm mirror. It does not shame the person. It shows what their bank statement has been trying to say.

## Repeatable Story Format

Use this structure when turning an idea into a post or video:

1. Open with the feeling.
2. Show the everyday pressure.
3. Let the character say what many people are thinking.
4. Reveal the hidden pattern in the bank statement.
5. Show the better decision MiFlujo makes possible.
6. End with the early-access CTA.

Example CTA lines:

- "Your bank statement has a story. MiFlujo helps you understand it."
- "Before you blame yourself, see the pattern."
- "Get clearer about your money before the next month starts."
- "Visit https://miflujo.taoforge.org to get early access."

## Story Ideas

### 1. Where Did My Salary Go?

**Persona:** Tolu, 29, works in customer support in Lagos. She is paid on the 25th and feels rich for about three days.

**Viewer expression:** "This is literally me after salary alert."

**Story:** Tolu receives her salary, pays rent balance, sends money home, buys groceries, renews data, settles one debt, pays for transport, and tells herself she will save next month. By the second week, she is checking her balance before buying lunch.

**Human moment:** She opens her banking app and whispers, "But I have not even done anything serious."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo shows that her money is not disappearing. It is being pulled by repeated transfers, food inflation, transport, subscriptions, and family support. Now she can plan with the real pattern, not vibes.

**Content formats:** POV reel, WhatsApp chat skit, carousel titled "Salary did not vanish. It had assignments."

### 2. Small Business Cashflow Confusion

**Persona:** Amaka, 34, runs a hair and beauty supply store. Sales are coming in, but cash never seems to stay.

**Viewer expression:** "Customers are buying, so why am I still broke?"

**Story:** Amaka posts new stock, gets transfers all week, and still struggles to restock on Friday. She assumes customers are owing too much, but the real issue is a mix of supplier price increases, delivery fees, POS charges, emergency withdrawals, and personal spending from the same account.

**Human moment:** She tells her friend, "I know I am selling. I just cannot prove I am making money."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps her see income, expenses, recurring costs, supplier payments, and leakage from the statement. The story becomes less about panic and more about control.

**Content formats:** Founder explainer, market-woman mini drama, before-and-after dashboard clip.

### 3. Family Support / Black Tax Tracking

**Persona:** Chinedu, 32, first son, software engineer, family emergency contact by default.

**Viewer expression:** "I want to help my people, but I also want to breathe."

**Story:** Chinedu sends money for his mother's medication, his brother's school project, his cousin's exam form, and an uncle's burial contribution. None of these payments feel wrong, but together they quietly reshape his whole month.

**Human moment:** He hesitates before opening another family message because he already knows what it may mean.

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps him understand how much family support affects his monthly cashflow without guilt. The point is not to stop helping; it is to help with awareness.

**Content formats:** Monologue, text-message reel, carousel titled "Generosity still needs a budget."

### 4. Subscription And Recurring Payment Creep

**Persona:** Zainab, 26, designer, heavy app user, always on a free trial that became paid.

**Viewer expression:** "Wait, I am still paying for that?"

**Story:** Zainab thinks she only spends on food and transport until MiFlujo surfaces streaming subscriptions, cloud storage, design tools, data renewals, loan app fees, and an app she downloaded once for a project.

**Human moment:** She sees one recurring payment and laughs because she cannot remember the password to the account.

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo surfaces repeated payments so she can decide what to keep, cancel, or renegotiate.

**Content formats:** Screen-record style reel, "subscriptions exposing me" skit, quick checklist post.

### 5. Couples Planning A Major Expense

**Persona:** Femi and Sade, engaged, planning rent, wedding, and furniture at the same time.

**Viewer expression:** "Love is sweet, but the spreadsheet is serious."

**Story:** They want a better apartment and a simple wedding, but every conversation about money becomes tense. Femi feels accused. Sade feels alone in planning. Neither of them has a shared view of what is actually coming in and going out.

**Human moment:** Sade says, "I am not asking because I do not trust you. I am asking because I do not want us to start stressed."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo gives them a clearer picture of current spending before they commit to a bigger obligation. It turns emotional guessing into a practical conversation.

**Content formats:** Couple dialogue reel, carousel for wedding/rent planning, relationship finance skit.

### 6. Lending Or Cooperative Contribution Story

**Persona:** Mrs. Adebayo, cooperative treasurer, responsible for approving member loans fairly.

**Viewer expression:** "Can this person really repay without suffering?"

**Story:** Members come with urgent needs: school fees, rent, business stock, medical bills. Mrs. Adebayo wants to help, but she also has to protect the cooperative's money. Reading bank statements manually is slow and stressful.

**Human moment:** She says, "It is not that I do not trust people. I just need to understand the pattern."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps review income stability, recurring deductions, spending pressure, and cashflow patterns faster.

**Content formats:** B2B explainer, cooperative meeting scene, loan officer POV.

### 7. Freelancer Income Instability

**Persona:** David, 28, video editor. One month he earns like a boss, the next month he is chasing invoices.

**Viewer expression:** "Freelance money can humble anybody."

**Story:** David gets paid for three projects in one week and upgrades his life immediately. Then two clients delay payment, his laptop needs repair, and rent is approaching. He realizes he plans from his best month, not his average month.

**Human moment:** He says, "The problem is not that I do not earn. The problem is that my income has mood swings."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo shows average income, peak months, low months, and spending habits so freelancers can plan around reality.

**Content formats:** Creator economy reel, invoice comedy skit, carousel titled "Your best month is not your budget."

### 8. School Fees Season Pressure

**Persona:** Mrs. Eze, mother of three, already disciplined with money until school fees season arrives.

**Viewer expression:** "School fees does not come alone."

**Story:** She budgets for school fees, but then uniforms, books, lunch packs, transport, extra lessons, PTA, and small school requests arrive one by one. The month feels abnormal, but she is not sure whether this is seasonal pressure or a bigger cashflow issue.

**Human moment:** She looks at the school list and says, "They said school fees, but this one is a full project."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps identify seasonal spikes and separate them from normal monthly spending patterns.

**Content formats:** Parent POV reel, back-to-school carousel, voiceover over receipts and transfer alerts.

### 9. Earning More But Still Not Saving

**Persona:** Kemi, 31, recently promoted. Her salary increased, but her savings stayed the same.

**Viewer expression:** "I upgraded my income and my problems upgraded too."

**Story:** Kemi starts earning more and expects relief. Instead, she moves to a better apartment, orders food more often, supports more people, changes her transport routine, and says yes to more outings. The raise is real, but so is the lifestyle creep.

**Human moment:** She checks her savings and says, "So what exactly did promotion do for me?"

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo reveals lifestyle creep, increased transfers, larger food or transport costs, and hidden recurring expenses.

**Content formats:** Salary raise skit, lifestyle creep carousel, quiet founder-led reflection.

### 10. Emergency Expenses Disrupting Everything

**Persona:** Musa, 37, family man, normally careful, thrown off by one unexpected repair.

**Viewer expression:** "One emergency can scatter the whole month."

**Story:** Musa's car breaks down, then his child needs medical tests. He covers both, but for the rest of the month every normal expense feels heavier. He wonders if he is bad with money, when the truth is that a one-off expense distorted the month.

**Human moment:** He says, "I was doing okay until life entered."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps separate unusual one-off expenses from normal spending patterns, so people do not judge their entire financial life by one hard month.

**Content formats:** Emotional short film, carousel on emergency spending, repair-bill POV.

### 11. Market Price Inflation

**Persona:** Bisi, 40, manages household spending and knows prices by memory.

**Viewer expression:** "The same money is not doing the same work again."

**Story:** Bisi used to shop comfortably with a familiar budget. Now rice, oil, fuel, transport, electricity, and cooking gas have changed the meaning of that amount. She feels like she is overspending, but the prices have moved.

**Human moment:** At the market, she asks, "How much?" then quietly removes two items from the list.

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps families see where costs have changed and revise the budget intelligently.

**Content formats:** Market trip reel, price comparison carousel, household budget story.

### 12. Shared Apartment / Roommate Expenses

**Persona:** Ire, 24, lives with two flatmates and somehow becomes the unofficial accountant.

**Viewer expression:** "Flatmate expenses are small until they become drama."

**Story:** Rent is shared, but internet, electricity, cleaning, repairs, water, and food replacement keep falling on Ire first. Everybody promises to send their share, but her account carries the pressure before the reimbursements arrive.

**Human moment:** She says, "I am not angry. I just need to know who has paid for what."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps track repeated shared expenses and see what keeps causing shortfalls.

**Content formats:** Flatmate group-chat skit, rent season carousel, young professional POV.

### 13. Loan Repayment Pressure

**Persona:** Kunle, 30, has a salary loan, a device installment, and two small loan-app deductions.

**Viewer expression:** "My salary arrives already wounded."

**Story:** Kunle's salary looks decent on paper, but deductions and repayments take their share before he starts living. He keeps wondering why every month feels tight.

**Human moment:** His salary alert comes in and he says, "This money has already been booked by other people."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo shows how much income is already committed to repayments before daily spending begins.

**Content formats:** Salary alert reel, debt pressure carousel, loan officer education post.

### 14. Religious And Community Giving

**Persona:** Ngozi, 35, active in church and community support, gives consistently and sincerely.

**Viewer expression:** "Giving matters to me, but I still need to know the number."

**Story:** Ngozi gives tithes, offerings, donations, welfare support, community dues, and occasional contributions. Each one is meaningful, but together they form a real part of her monthly spending.

**Human moment:** She says, "I do not regret giving. I just want to give wisely."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps users understand the percentage of income going to giving and support, without treating generosity as a mistake.

**Content formats:** Reflective founder post, faith/community carousel, gentle monologue.

### 15. Mixing Personal And Business Money

**Persona:** Hassan, 33, runs a food delivery business from one bank account.

**Viewer expression:** "My business account and my personal life are fighting inside one statement."

**Story:** Customer payments, rider fees, supplier payments, groceries, family transfers, fuel, ads, and personal withdrawals all pass through the same account. Hassan is busy and hardworking, but his statement reads like five different stories at once.

**Human moment:** His accountant asks for records and he says, "Everything is there. It is just not arranged."

**MiFlujo angle:** MiFlujo helps separate the story and show what is really happening, especially for founders who have not fully separated accounts yet.

**Content formats:** Founder skit, small business carousel, accountant conversation reel.

## Fictional Content Series Concepts

### Series 1: The Statement Knows

A recurring short-form series where different people open their bank statement and discover the pattern behind a money problem they thought was personal failure.

Episodes:

- Tolu: salary disappearing.
- Amaka: business selling but cash missing.
- Chinedu: family support pressure.
- David: freelancer income swings.
- Kemi: earning more but not saving.

Tone: warm, slightly funny, emotionally honest.

### Series 2: Money Conversations People Avoid

Two-character scenes where one person finally says the financial thing they have been hiding or avoiding.

Episodes:

- Couple planning rent before marriage.
- Flatmates arguing over shared bills.
- First son admitting family support is heavy.
- Business owner telling a friend sales are not the same as profit.
- Parent admitting school fees season is bigger than expected.

Tone: intimate, human, non-judgmental.

### Series 3: Before You Blame Yourself

Founder-led or voiceover content that reframes common financial shame.

Episode pattern:

1. "Before you say you are bad with money..."
2. Name the hidden pattern.
3. Explain what a bank statement can reveal.
4. Show how MiFlujo helps.

Tone: calm, educational, reassuring.

## Strongest Nigerian Relatability Angles

- Salary disappearing before month-end.
- Family support / black tax.
- School fees season pressure.
- Market price inflation.
- Small business cashflow confusion.
- Mixing personal and business money.
- Loan repayment pressure.
- Earning more but still not saving.

## Writing Notes

- Keep stories warm, practical, and non-judgmental.
- Make the person feel seen before introducing the product.
- Avoid making users look careless, foolish, or irresponsible.
- Do not frame family support, giving, or emergencies as bad decisions.
- Let MiFlujo provide clarity, not moral correction.
- Use bank statements as the source of truth: patterns, categories, repeated payments, income stability, and one-off events.
- End with one clear CTA: `https://miflujo.taoforge.org`.
